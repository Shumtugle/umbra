# Changes

## 0.3

- The station: a table on a glass deck of a station turning in orbit. Nothing
  floats over the net; the turning room itself curves every ball, the same way
  from either end. Through the glass the stars and the planet wheel round at the
  very rate of the spin, so the ball turns the way the sky turns. The grid on
  the table is twisted, and lanes of light trace the curve of a ball each way.
- The realms go in order: the hole, the station, the nucleus.
- Levels are kept by the realm's name, so adding realms never mixes them up.

## 0.2

- Realms: a world, the law by which its thing moves the ball, and the signs
  that show that law. The title screen chooses the realm with a sideways swipe.
- The nucleus, 1911: a laboratory with a board floor, a lamp and a blackboard;
  an atomic nucleus over the net pushes the ball away. Its own hum: mains and
  sparks.
- Each realm keeps its own level, and its force grows with the level. The grid
  on the table bends more with it, and never folds over itself.
- The black hole's funnel is deeper and wider.

## 0.1

The first build.

- A black hole over the net: it pulls the ball and swallows what crosses its
  horizon. The light bends around it; the grid on the table falls toward it.
- The table fills the screen; every game pixel is the same whole number of
  screen pixels.
- A real net: the mesh stops the ball, the cord deflects it, a serve that
  touches it is played again. Hard or careless shots can end in it.
- Synthesized sound: paddle, table, net, cord, the fall into the hole, chimes,
  and the hum of the hole, which grows as the ball comes near.
- The first level is gentle; the opponent misses, nets and overhits.
