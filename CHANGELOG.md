# Changes

## 0.9

- The darkroom, 1839: our half of the table lies under a red safelight, the far
  half is the easel under the enlarger, and whatever is over there, the far half,
  the opponent and the ball, is seen as an image through one of thirty-one eyes.
  The image is made at the depth of the net, so a ball on this side is always
  seen as it is. The instruments: a negative, a daguerreotype, a cyanotype, old
  sepia, a halftone screen, a mosaic, a heat camera, X-rays, a colour-blind eye,
  a long exposure. The painters: Van Gogh, Monet, Manet, Renoir, Degas, Cézanne,
  Gauguin, Seurat, Matisse, Chagall, Malevich, Klimt, Munch, Kandinsky, Picasso,
  Dalí, Miró, Magritte, Modigliani, Klee, Pollock.
- Realms that come in versions: each match takes one version from a shuffled
  bag of its own, kept between sessions, so none comes back before all have
  been seen; a match left unfinished keeps its version. The story button shows
  the version's own card before the realm's essay.
- New exhibits in the cabinet: a cloud chamber with its two curling tracks, a
  plate with two slits and its fringes, a plate camera.
- A darkroom's sound: water running through the wash tray, the safelight's
  transformer, a drop now and then.

## 0.8

- The double slit, 1927: in place of the net, a screen of smoked glass with two
  slits; a ball must pass through one of them, and the glass stops the rest.
  Behind it bright and dark fringes lie across the table, and the ball is drawn
  to the nearest bright one. A lamp over the left slit lights up every so
  often: while it watches, the fringes fade and the ball flies plain. Every
  landing leaves a dot, and the dots build up the fringes over a match. In the
  deeper rounds a shadow ball from the world next door flies through the other
  slit.

## 0.7

- The cloud chamber, 1932: vapour, a lamp from the side, black velvet, and two
  coils that fill the middle with a magnetic field. Every struck ball carries
  a charge, red or blue, written beside it; in the field red turns one way and
  blue the other. The field shows as dots on the table, densest where it is
  strongest; the ball leaves a trail of mist that shows its curve; stray
  particles spiral across the table by the same law. Vapour hiss and a counter
  clicking.
- Bigger everything a finger or an eye has to find: the ball is drawn half
  again as large, the story button fits a thumb, and the score, names and
  hints are twice the size.

## 0.6

- Each realm is named at the top of the screen, with the number of the round
  under it; the strength of the opponent still follows the results, unseen.
- A question mark in the top right corner opens the realm's story: what the
  experiment was, its history, its idea and its people. The stories are being
  written; a short one stands in for each until then.
- The cabinet of curiosities: every match won leaves an exhibit of its realm
  under a glass dome, with the score on its plate. Won with the opponent on
  three points or fewer, it stands on gold; won from five points down, it wears
  a red ribbon. The last exhibits stand on a shelf on the title screen; touch
  the shelf to open the whole cabinet.
- A match left half played waits on the title screen and goes on from the same
  score, even after the game is closed.
- The law of the realm is named at the start of every match.

## 0.5

- The Black River, 1837: a winter evening by a frozen river, snow, firs, a
  sledge and a lantern; a duel fought with paddles, where nobody falls.
- The law is the wind: it carries the ball sideways, turning from one side to
  the other, and every few seconds a gust crosses the table as a travelling
  wave. Falling snow drifts and streaks with it, and the gust's front sweeps the
  table as a band of driven snow before it reaches the ball.
- The barrier: a paddle that comes forward to its barrier line, marked in red
  on the table, strikes up to a quarter harder.
- One shot each a match, shown as a small pistol by the score: a forward swipe
  faster than any ordinary stroke fires the hardest, deepest ball there is, low
  over the net, with a crack and a cloud of smoke that the wind carries away.

## 0.4

- The Lyceum, 335 BC: the physics of the ancients, played as if it were true.
  A struck ball flies in a straight line while its impetus lasts, then falls
  straight down to its natural place. The harder the swing, the faster and
  flatter the line. A line to where the impetus ends, a plumb line and a ring
  on the table show where the ball will fall; a ring round the ball shrinks as
  the impetus is spent. Under the table, the cosmos in mosaic. Cicadas.
- Realms come in a shuffled order: every visit begins at the hole, then each
  match is played in another realm, all of them before any comes twice. One
  level for the whole game again.
- Paddles belong to their realm: black edges in space, composite and safety
  orange on the station, cork and brass in the laboratory, painted olive wood
  in the Lyceum.

## 0.3

- The station: a table on a glass deck of a station turning in orbit. Nothing
  floats over the net; the turning room itself curves every ball, the same way
  from either end. Through the glass the stars and the planet wheel round at the
  very rate of the spin, so the ball turns the way the sky turns. The grid on
  the table is twisted, and lanes of light trace the curve of a ball each way.
- The realms go in order: the hole, the station, the nucleus.
- Levels are kept by the realm's name, so adding realms never mixes them up.

## 0.2

- Realms: a world, the law by which its thing moves the ball, and the signs
  that show that law. The title screen chooses the realm with a sideways swipe.
- The nucleus, 1911: a laboratory with a board floor, a lamp and a blackboard;
  an atomic nucleus over the net pushes the ball away. Its own hum: mains and
  sparks.
- Each realm keeps its own level, and its force grows with the level. The grid
  on the table bends more with it, and never folds over itself.
- The black hole's funnel is deeper and wider.

## 0.1

The first build.

- A black hole over the net: it pulls the ball and swallows what crosses its
  horizon. The light bends around it; the grid on the table falls toward it.
- The table fills the screen; every game pixel is the same whole number of
  screen pixels.
- A real net: the mesh stops the ball, the cord deflects it, a serve that
  touches it is played again. Hard or careless shots can end in it.
- Synthesized sound: paddle, table, net, cord, the fall into the hole, chimes,
  and the hum of the hole, which grows as the ball comes near.
- The first level is gentle; the opponent misses, nets and overhits.
